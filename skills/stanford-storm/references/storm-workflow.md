# Stanford STORM Workflow Notes

Use this reference when applying STORM mode or when the user asks what makes the workflow different from ordinary outlining.

## Core Idea

STORM means Synthesis of Topic Outlines through Retrieval and Multi-perspective Question Asking. Use it as a research-first workflow for long-form, source-grounded writing:

1. Discover diverse perspectives for the topic.
2. Ask questions from each perspective.
3. Ground answers in retrieved or supplied sources.
4. Curate the findings into an outline.
5. Generate or polish the article from the outline and references.

## Practical Adaptation For Codex

Codex usually should not claim to run the official STORM engine unless it has actually installed and executed it. For ordinary chat work, emulate the workflow:

- Perspective discovery: identify angles that would lead to different research questions.
- Question asking: write specific questions and follow-ups for each angle.
- Retrieval grounding: browse or use provided sources when facts need support.
- Source ledger: keep evidence traceable from claim to source.
- Outline synthesis: build the article structure after research, not before.
- Drafting: write neutral, citation-aware prose from the outline.

## Perspective Patterns

Choose perspectives that fit the topic. Common useful sets:

- Technology topic: architecture, users, economics, security, regulation, ecosystem, limitations, future work.
- Company topic: history, products, market, financials, leadership, competitors, controversies, outlook.
- Policy topic: legal basis, affected stakeholders, implementation, costs, benefits, risks, opposition, open questions.
- Scientific topic: background, methods, evidence, limitations, competing interpretations, applications, safety, future research.

## Quality Bar

- Prefer fewer, stronger citations over many weak ones.
- Separate evidence from interpretation.
- Preserve uncertainty and source disagreement.
- Do not over-associate unrelated facts because they appeared in nearby search results.
- Watch for source bias transfer: a biased source can make neutral prose biased even when paraphrased.
- Do not fabricate bibliographic details, quotations, publication dates, or statistics.

## When To Browse

Browse when the user asks for latest information, the topic is time-sensitive, the claim is high-stakes, or sources are required and not provided. Use primary or authoritative sources where possible.
