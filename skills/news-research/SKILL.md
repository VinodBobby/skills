# Purpose

Explain what is known about a news event or claim, how the evidence developed, and what remains uncertain.

# Workflow

## Frame the question

Identify the claim or event, relevant people or organizations, place, and time window. For recent events, search current sources and distinguish the event date from each report's publication or update date. State the search window and any material limits on coverage.

## Scan and drill down

Search across the main entities, the specific claim, likely primary records, and independent reporting. Use results to discover sources, then inspect the underlying pages or documents before relying on them.

Prefer original records where available: official statements, filings, court records, agency notices, transcripts, datasets, or direct testimony. Compare them with independent reporting. Check whether apparently separate reports rely on the same wire story or source; repetition alone is not corroboration.

For disputed or consequential claims, seek independent confirmation and inspect evidence that could contradict the leading account. Keep each source's date, proximity to the event, and known limitations in view.

## Sort the evidence

Classify important statements as confirmed by primary evidence, reported but not independently confirmed, allegation, analysis or forecast, or unresolved. Do not turn an attributed claim into an established fact. Preserve meaningful disagreements between sources and explain what evidence supports each account.

Build a chronology when sequence matters. Tie each event to its date and source, and mark corrections or changes in the account.

## Report

Lead with the best-supported answer. Then provide a concise timeline, a claim-and-evidence table with source links and dates, disputed or unverified points, and unanswered questions. Put citations next to the claims they support. Paraphrase sources; quote only when the wording itself matters.

For breaking news, label early information as preliminary and update the conclusion only when new evidence supports it. Never imply that a search was exhaustive when it was not.

# Requirements

Use current search or browsing tools for current events. If they are unavailable, work from user-provided material, state the information limit, and do not present stale knowledge as current reporting.
