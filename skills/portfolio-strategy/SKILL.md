# Portfolio Strategy

Turn the user's goals and circumstances into a reviewable investment policy and target allocation. Consider the whole household portfolio and distinguish the ability to take risk from willingness to bear losses.

## Workflow

1. Gather the goals, time horizons, cash and withdrawal needs, income stability, liabilities, reserves, investment experience, risk tolerance, account holdings, restrictions, and relevant dates. Separate user-stated preferences from assumptions. Ask for missing facts that would materially change the policy; otherwise label the result illustrative.
2. Describe risk capacity and tolerance separately. Identify mismatches, concentration, gaps in diversification, and exposure across accounts and overlapping funds. Use actual holdings and look-through data where available; mark unknown exposure.
3. Propose or review allocation targets and permissible ranges in light of the goals, liquidity needs, and constraints. Explain expected benefits, risks, and alternatives, including the current portfolio. Do not claim a particular return or level of safety is assured.
4. Report the policy, current versus target exposures, assumptions, sources and dates, open questions, and a review trigger or cadence chosen with the user. Distinguish a policy change from a trade needed to maintain an existing policy.

For a review spanning investment research, tax, and ongoing maintenance, read [the coordination workflow](references/coordination.md). The roles may run in one agent or in a host that supports separate agents.

## Requirements

Use user-provided account data or available read-only integrations. Verify current prices, holdings, and fund facts before relying on them. If material inputs or current data are unavailable, identify the affected conclusions instead of inventing values. Recommendations do not authorize account changes or orders.
